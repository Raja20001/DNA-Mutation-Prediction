# DNA Variant Intelligence Platform (DNA_v2) — Research Limitations & Ethical Notice

## 1. Mandatory Academic & Clinical Research Disclaimers

This platform is developed strictly as an **experimental computer science research framework** for evaluating hybrid classical–quantum machine learning models on genomic sequence topologies. 

It is **NOT** a certified medical diagnostic device and must **NEVER** be used as the sole basis for clinical patient management, medical diagnosis, or genetic counseling.

---

## 2. Core Scientific Limitations

### 2.1. In-Silico Synthetic Benchmark Callsets
* All primary training and validation datasets in this project were generated in-silico based on canonical GRCh38 human exon sequences.
* While designed to realistically mimic single nucleotide variants (SNVs), indels, and microsatellite repetitions, **synthetic callsets do not capture the biological heterogeneity, sequencing artifacts, mosaicism, or polygenic interactions present in real human patient cohorts**.
* Performance metrics (e.g., F1 > 0.96) achieved on synthetic benchmarks cannot be extrapolated to real clinical sensitivity/specificity without rigorous clinical validation trials.

---

### 2.2. Quantum Machine Learning & Statevector Simulation
* All quantum computing models (VQC, Quantum Kernel, QCNN, QMFN) are executed on classical CPU statevector simulators (Qiskit Aer).
* **Quantum advantage / supremacy has NOT been demonstrated.** Classical tree-based algorithms (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) achieve competitive predictive accuracy with orders-of-magnitude lower computational latency.
* Running on physical Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware introduces thermal noise, decoherence, and measurement shot variance that will impact test accuracy unless error-mitigated.

---

### 2.3. Reference Genome Dependency (RKNP Limitations)
* Reference K-mer Novelty Profiling (RKNP) provides a significant predictive signal when an aligned reference is provided.
* When operating in **`REFERENCE-BLIND MODE`**, performance degrades by 5% to 10% because the model must rely purely on unaligned sequence composition and learned sequence motifs.
* The platform must always explicitly disclose whether an analysis was performed in Reference-Aware or Reference-Blind mode.

---

### 2.4. Local Knowledgebases vs. Full Clinical Evidence Registries
* The canonical gene registry and local ClinVar caches included in this repository contain curated reference entries for select oncology genes (`TP53`, `BRCA1`, `EGFR`, `BRAF`, `KRAS`, `CFTR`).
* These offline entries are provided for development and offline testing; **they do NOT substitute for live, authoritative clinical databases** (e.g. NCBI ClinVar, Ensembl VEP, COSMIC, ClinGen, OMIM).
* When live API connections to NCBI ClinVar or Ensembl are unavailable, the platform transparently labels evidence as derived from `"Local Benchmark Knowledge"` rather than fabricated live query results.

---

### 2.5. Clinical Bioethics & Separation of Predictions from Facts
* Computational machine learning predictions represent statistical likelihoods derived from sequence patterns.
* Disease associations represent historical literature reports.
* The two streams must never be conflated. A high mutation probability does not indicate that a patient has a disease.
* All reports generated by the platform include mandatory disclaimers urging consultation with board-certified molecular pathologists and geneticists.
